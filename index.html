When did it start?

Not always chest pain

Many heart attacks start slowly, with mild pain or discomfort. Women are more likely than men to have shortness of breath, nausea or vomiting, and back or jaw pain, with or without chest pain.

If you think it might be a heart attack, it's always right to call.

    Methods and validation

    How Restart works

    Everything runs in the browser. Video never leaves the phone. The numbers on this page are computed live, by the same code the coach uses.

    01 · Track

    MediaPipe Pose finds 33 body points in each camera frame, on the device. Restart uses shoulders, elbows, wrists and hips.

    02 · Find each push

    Wrist height is measured against the hips, which barely move, cancelling camera shake. A hysteresis detector marks every top and bottom.

    03 · Measure

    The rescuer's own arm is the ruler: shoulder to wrist is about 57 cm. Rate from the time between pushes; recoil from how far the hands rise back to their resting height.

    04 · Coach

    One correction at a time, in priority order: keep pushing, rate, depth, elbows, recoil. Spoken, on screen, and with a 110/min metronome.

    Live validation

    Simulated rescuers with known technique, including camera shake, a slow 15 fps phone, near and far cameras, and mistakes. The coach should measure the truth and give the right correction.

    Heart attack check rules

    Built on the American Heart Association's warning signs. It always errs toward calling.

    SituationAdvice
    Unresponsive, not breathing normally or only gaspingCall and start CPR
    Any chest pain, pressure, squeezing or fullnessCall now
    Two or more other warning signsCall now
    One sign, plus a risk factor or age 50+Call now
    One sign, no risk factorsCan't rule out: call if new

    Never: "drive to hospital", an aspirin dose, or "you're fine".

    Targets (AHA 2025, adults)

    • Rate 100–120 compressions per minute.
    • Depth at least 5 cm, no more than 6 cm.
    • Recoil let the chest come all the way back up; don't lean.
    • Pauses as few and as short as possible, under 10 seconds.
    • Swap rescuers about every 2 minutes; quality drops with tiredness.

    Honest limits

    Depth from one camera is approximate (about ±1 cm), because it assumes an average arm length and a side view. It is tested on simulated rescuers, not yet on manikins with depth sensors, which is the next step. Restart is a prototype, not a medical device. It never delays compressions: the metronome and voice start before the camera does, and work without it.

    01 · The problem

    Ischaemic heart disease kills more people than anything else.

    9Mdeaths in 2021, about 13% of all deaths worldwideWHO, 2024
    90%of the ~350,000 out-of-hospital cardiac arrests in the US each year are fatalAmerican Heart Association
    2–3×higher survival when a bystander starts CPR straight awayAmerican Heart Association

    02 · The gap

    The help is there. People hesitate, or do it wrong.

    • Heart attacks get waited out. Symptoms are often mild, and without chest pain in many women, older people and people with diabetes.
    • Most arrests get no CPR. People don't act more than half the time; they freeze, or fear doing it wrong.
    • CPR that is done is often too slow or too shallow, and gets worse within two minutes as the rescuer tires. Nobody is there to say so.

    04 · What's new

    A CPR feedback device, without the device.

    • Your arm is the ruler: depth from a single camera, scaled by the rescuer's own shoulder-to-wrist length.
    • Hips as an anchor: wrist motion measured against the hips cancels a shaky, propped-up phone.
    • Never delays a push: metronome and voice start instantly; the camera joins when it's ready.
    • Practice that sticks: a one-minute pillow session scored out of 100, any time, for free.
